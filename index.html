    02 — Contract structure & churn

    05 — Tenure hazard curve

    Churn drops from 53% at onboarding to 7% by year six.

    Customer tenure is the single strongest continuous predictor of survival. In months 0–6 (1,481 accounts), 52.9% of subscribers leave; between months 7 and 12, attrition eases to 35.9%.

    By Year 2 (13–24 months) churn falls to 28.7%, declining steadily through 19.0% in Year 4 and bottoming out at 6.6% among 1,407 six-year accounts.

    11 — ML prediction, lift & uplift

    Target persuadable churners, not sure things.

    Two scikit-learn-equivalent classifiers train live on a stratified 80/20 split of all 7,043 rows: an L2-regularized Logistic Regression (C=1.0, IRLS) and a Gradient Boosted Decision Tree ensemble (45 trees, depth 3).

    Predictive Lift ranks accounts by baseline churn probability to concentrate outreach in high-risk deciles—capturing over half of all exits inside the top 20%.

    Counterfactual Uplift (τ = Pbaseline − Poffer) measures incremental churn reduction from management’s retention offer, separating high-ROI Persuadables from low-risk Sure Things.

    12 — Causal observational study · Propensity score matching

    Did Fiber’s non-RCT campaign deliver a 1-year net ROI?

    One year ago, management pushed Fiber optic upgrades (n = 3,096) without running an A/B test or randomized controlled trial (RCT). Because there was no counterfactual—we cannot observe the same customer simultaneously on Fiber and DSL—we compare upgraded Fiber users against DSL users (n = 2,421). Raw numbers make Fiber look +22.9 percentage points (pp) worse on churn (41.9% vs 19.0%), alongside +$33.40/mo higher bills.

    However, the Fiber push heavily targeted customers already prone to leaving: month-to-month, paperless, electronic-check, and unbundled accounts (max |SMD| = 0.728). Comparing those groups raw mixes up who was pitched with what Fiber actually did (Rosenbaum & Rubin, 1983; Rosenbaum, 2010).

    To construct an apples-to-apples benchmark (Rosenbaum, 2010, Ch. 18), we exclude post-upgrade pricing (MonthlyCharges) and pair each Fiber upgrade 1:1 with a statistically similar DSL twin using a propensity caliper c · SD(logit ê) (Rosenbaum & Rubin, 1985). Matching cuts the apparent churn gap nearly in half—leaving a true causal effect of roughly +11 to +12 pp more churn, not less.

    Because an observational study can never rule out unrecorded factors (e.g. rival promos or outages), we run Rosenbaum sensitivity bounds (Γ) (Rosenbaum, 2002, Ch. 4; 2017) to measure how large an invisible bias would have to be to overturn our ROI and churn conclusions.
